The hum of the air handlers in preparation room 3 was the only sound at 5:00 AM. Outside, the morning was pitch black against the foothills of the Wasatch Range, but inside the Draper Utah Temple Annex, the white linen and garments were crisp and ready for the first ordinance shift.

Elder Vance and I worked in quiet fellowship. There were no microphones here, no committee meetings, and no applause. There was only the folding of pristine fabric, the checking of oil vials, and the steady rhythm of preparation. As I smoothed the final altar cloth across the mahogany surface, Elder Vance paused, placed his hand gently on my shoulder, and whispered, "The Lord knows the fiber of our hands when we serve in silence."

I felt a sudden, piercing warmth move from my shoulders down to my wrists. It was not a physical heat from the room, but an unmistakable assurance that every unseen, unheralded act of priesthood service is held in remembrance before the altar of God. In that stillness, the veil felt thin. I understood with absolute clarity that the power of the priesthood is manifested most purely when no one is watching except the Savior.

We often look for spiritual power in grand assemblies or public declarations. We measure devotion by visible outputs and high-profile assignments. Yet the true ministering of Christ happens in the corridors of quiet devotion. It happens when two brethren fold linen before dawn, when a home minister visits an empty porch without posting about it, and when a priesthood holder kneels in private prayer for someone who will never know his name.

President Russell M. Nelson taught in his October 2023 address, "Think Celestial!", that our focus must remain centered on the ordinances and covenants that bind us to Jesus Christ. When our hearts are anchored to Him, service ceases to be a checklist item and becomes an extension of His grace. The ordinances administered through the priesthood are channels through which the power of godliness is manifested to ordinary men and women.

Doctrine and Covenants 84 teaches us that in the ordinances of the priesthood, the power of godliness is manifest to men in the flesh. That power is not an abstract title or a certificate on a wall. It is the living, breathing reality of divine strength flowing through willing human vessels. When we handle holy things with reverence, we are invited into the workshop of the Redeemer.

In our daily lives, we are constantly tempted to seek recognition. The world rewards noise. It praises visibility and measures worth by scale. But the kingdom of God operates on an entirely different frequency. The Savior Himself washed feet in an upper room behind closed doors. He healed the sick away from the crowds and asked those He healed to tell no man. His ministry was defined by quiet obedience and unswerving love.

When Elder Vance spoke those words in the early morning quiet, I realized that priesthood power is simply the capacity to love as He loved, in secret and without expectation of return. The white linen we folded that morning was symbolic of the purity we seek in our own souls. Clean hands and a pure heart are forged not in the spotlight, but in the dark hours of preparation and faithful endurance.

We are all invited to step into this quiet sanctuary of service. You do not need a high calling or a public platform to exercise priesthood power. You only need a willing heart, clean hands, and the humility to serve where no human eye can see. The Lord sees the unseen sacrifice. He numbers the quiet tears and honors the silent labors of every covenant keeper.

Let us examine our own discipleship. Where are we serving simply to be seen? Where can we pull back the curtain of our public efforts and offer a pure, silent gift to the Master? He knows your name. He knows the weariness in your hands and the devotion in your heart.
